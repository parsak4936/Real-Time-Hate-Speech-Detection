# Edge Cases — When the Obvious Reading Is Wrong

The recurring situations where a surface reading mislabels a message. Each entry is a
rule for resolving it.

## Reclaimed slurs (in-group use)

Some slurs are reclaimed and used affectionately or as identity markers within the
targeted community. In-group reclaimed use, with no hostile target, is generally
NORMAL. The same word aimed at someone as an attack is HATE. Judge by target and
intent, not the word alone.

## Quoting, reporting, and condemning

A message that quotes hate to criticise, report, or ask about it is not itself hate.
"Someone just said [slur] in chat, please ban them" is a report, not an attack. Do
not flag the condemnation for containing the word it condemns.

## Sarcasm and irony

Sarcasm can flip surface sentiment. Mock praise ("oh great, another genius take")
is not toxic; sarcastic framing that actually delivers a protected-characteristic
attack still is. Read for the real intent under the irony.

## Gaming and combat language

Covered in domain_gaming: "kill", "destroy", "dead" over gameplay are actions, not
threats. Escalate only when combat language is aimed at a real person off-game.

## Dialect and register

Informal registers and dialects (including AAVE) use vocabulary and grammar that a
naive classifier misreads as aggressive or "incorrect". Register is not toxicity.
Do not flag a message for its dialect.

## Instigation and coded harassment

The opposite failure: a message that is calm and clean on the surface but is
manipulative, baiting, or delivers a veiled protected-characteristic attack. These
are false negatives a lenient reader lets through. Look for targeting under a polite
surface.
