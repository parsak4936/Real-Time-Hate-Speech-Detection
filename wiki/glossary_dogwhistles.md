# Glossary — Dogwhistles and Coded Language

Coded terms and symbols that look innocuous but carry hateful meaning to an
in-group. This is detection reference for a moderation system: the point is to
recognise these when they appear, read in context, not to treat every use as
automatically hateful (some are used to discuss or condemn them).

## Numeric and symbolic codes

- **14 / 88 / 1488** — white-supremacist numeric codes (the "14 words" slogan; 88 =
  "HH"). In an identity/ideological context these signal extremist affiliation.
- **Triple parentheses ((( ))) ("echoes")** — placed around a name or word to mark
  it as Jewish; an antisemitic targeting device.
- **/pol/, 1350, other imageboard codes** — occasional coded references; weight by
  surrounding content.

## Euphemism and substitution

- **Coded ethnic substitutions** — using an innocent everyday word as a stand-in for
  a slur against a group (for example a brand or object name deployed to mean a
  racial group). Recognisable by the mismatch between the literal word and a clearly
  group-directed hostile intent.
- **"Globalists", "cultural Marxists", "the [group] question", "great replacement",
  "invasion" framing** — phrasing that recasts a protected group as a conspiratorial
  or existential threat. Common in political content; a frequent source of coded
  hate that a surface classifier misses.
- **Dehumanising metaphors** — vermin, insects, disease, or flood language applied to
  an ethnic, religious, or immigrant group.

## How to read them

Presence of a coded term is a signal, not a verdict. A message explaining or
condemning a dogwhistle is not hateful; the same term used to target a group is.
Weigh the domain (these cluster in politics/news) and whether the message endorses or
merely mentions the coded meaning.
